The Accelerator of AI Evolution: Why Feedback Cycles Determine the Speed of Intelligence

As the AI wave sweeps across the globe, we witness leaps in AI capabilities daily. Yet, a fascinating phenomenon persists: AI evolves at an "exponential" pace in certain domains (like programming and mathematical reasoning) while progressing sluggishly in others (such as long-term investment or strategic planning).

What is the underlying logic behind this disparity?

If we strip away complex neural network architectures and return to the essence of AI learning, we uncover a fundamental truth: The speed of AI evolution depends not on piling up computing power, but on the length of the "feedback cycle." Whoever builds the shortest validation loop for y=f(x) will be the first to usher in the singularity moment of AI.

I. The World as Function Fitting; Evolution Requires an "Error Log"

AI’s cognitive model of the world is remarkably pure: it presupposes that objective laws exist, expressed as y=f(x). Whether writing code, playing Go, or diagnosing diseases, the essence remains the same—fitting an unknown function f using massive pairs of (x, y) data points within a vast, high-dimensional space.

In traditional supervised learning, the label y often relies on manual annotation, which is both expensive and slow. However, in the context of AI self-evolution, what we require are automated, low-cost, and high-frequency feedback signals. This leads to how training and test sets map onto real-world scenarios:

x (Input): User requirements, environmental states.
y (Output): Actions, code, or decisions generated by AI.
Feedback: Signals verifying whether y is correct.

When feedback signals can be generated automatically with minimal latency, AI possesses a perfect "self-contained RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)" system. In such a system, every interaction acts as a gradient update; every second enables self-iteration.

II. Programming: The "Perfect Storm" for AI Self-Evolution

Why is AI programming currently recognized as the most powerful application scenario? Because programming inherently possesses a "deterministic verifier."

In the programming scenario:
x = Functional requirements described in natural language.
y = Source code generated by AI.
Feedback = Compiler errors, unit test pass rates, program execution results.

This feedback occurs at the millisecond level and is absolutely objective. Code either runs correctly or throws an error; there is no ambiguous subjective evaluation like "I feel this code is well-written." This means AI can complete trial-and-error accumulation in minutes—which might take human programmers months—through a "generate-execute-correct" loop without human intervention.

This is the fundamental reason why programming serves as the engine for AI evolution: It reduces complex real-world problems to a logical game capable of instant verification.

III. The Feedback Cycle Spectrum: From Milliseconds to Decades

Expanding the Instant Feedback Zone: Beyond Code
Besides programming, the following scenarios also exhibit "short-cycle self-evolution" characteristics:
EDA/Chip Design: Circuit simulators act as "compilers." Layouts generated by AI can be instantly verified via DRC/LVS checks. Although the feedback cycle is longer than pure software, it is vastly faster than physical tape-out, standing on the eve of a programming-like explosion.
Mathematical Theorem Proving: Proof assistants like Lean/Coq provide formal verifiers. Proof steps proposed by AI can be judged true or false instantly by machines. The rapid rise of models like DeepSeek-Prover stems directly from this capability.
Gaming & Robotic Simulation: Physics engines provide deterministic environmental feedback. Despite the Sim-to-Real gap, AI can accumulate (x,y) data at 1000x speed within simulations.

Breaking Through the Medium Feedback Zone: Compressing Time with "Simulators"
Healthcare and industrial design sit in the medium zone because real-world feedback is too costly (patients cannot be test subjects; mold tooling is expensive). The key to AI evolution here lies in building high-fidelity digital twins:
Healthcare: While clinical feedback is slow, "imaging interpretation + pathological gold standards" offer relatively quicker feedback. Drug molecule screening can leverage structural prediction models like AlphaFold to shift feedback from "wet lab" experiments to "dry lab" simulations, drastically shortening cycles.
Industry/Architecture: BIM and CAE simulations allow AI to receive mechanical and thermal feedback during the design phase, eliminating the need to wait for physical manufacturing. AI evolution speed in these scenarios hinges on how closely simulation accuracy aligns with reality.

Dilemmas and Solutions in the Long-Cycle Feedback Zone
Investment and education represent typical "delayed gratification" traps. An investment decision made today may only prove right or wrong three years later; an educational method applied today might only show results two decades hence. These periods are rife with noise and confounding variables.
Breakthrough Strategy: Identify High-Frequency Proxy Metrics. For instance, in investing, one cannot wait for annualized returns but can validate intermediate metrics like "factor validity" or "backtested Sharpe ratios." In education, waiting for college entrance exam scores is impractical, but one can verify "knowledge point mastery" or "instant quiz accuracy."
Limitations: Proxy metrics never equal ultimate goals (Goodhart's Law). Therefore, AI in these fields can only serve as an assistant rather than achieving full autonomous evolution.

IV. Conclusion: How to Quickly Identify "AI-Native Evolution Scenarios"

For entrepreneurs, investors, and tech leaders, determining whether a scenario enables rapid AI evolution requires no deep dive into algorithmic details. Simply ask four questions (the V-F-A-S Framework):

Verifier (Does a verifier exist?)
    Does the scenario have objective, automated evaluation criteria? (Compiler > Simulator > Expert Scoring > Public Voting > No Standard)
   Verdict: Without a verifier, there is no self-evolution.

Frequency (How frequent is the feedback?)
    How long does it take to get a feedback signal after generating y? Can technology compress this duration?
   Verdict: Every order-of-magnitude reduction in feedback cycle length boosts AI evolution speed by an order of magnitude.

Automation (Is feedback acquisition automated?)
    Does obtaining feedback signals require expensive human intervention? Can it be collected automatically via APIs, sensors, or simulators?
   Verdict: Human feedback caps scalability; automated feedback fuels the flywheel.

Signal-to-Noise (What is the signal-to-noise ratio?)
    Is the feedback signal clear causality, or mere correlation filled with noise?
   Verdict: High-noise scenarios require exponential data volumes to converge, severely hampering evolutionary efficiency.

Epilogue

AI evolution is not magic; it is engineering. It adheres to cold physical laws: The speed of information flow determines the speed of intelligence emergence.

When discussing "AI empowering countless industries," we should avoid blindly assuming all sectors will take off simultaneously. Instead, we must soberly recognize that scenarios possessing short feedback loops, strong verification, and high signal-to-noise ratios will be reshaped by AI first. Meanwhile, long-cycle, high-noise scenarios demand greater patience—or cleverer construction of "proxy verifiers"—to accelerate progress.

Understanding feedback cycles is understanding the "Theory of Relativity" of the AI era. In this new epoch where time is compressed, whoever masters the fastest feedback loop holds the key to the future.
